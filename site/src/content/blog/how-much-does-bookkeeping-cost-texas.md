If you've ever Googled "how much does a bookkeeper cost," you've probably found answers ranging from $50 a month to $5,000 a month — which is about as helpful as a weather forecast that says "somewhere between freezing and 110 degrees." Welcome to Texas.

The honest answer is that bookkeeping pricing depends on what your business actually needs. But "it depends" isn't a useful answer either, so in this guide we'll break down the real ranges Texas small businesses typically pay in 2026, what drives the price up or down, and how to figure out which tier you belong in — whether you run a construction crew in Fort Worth, a restaurant in Austin, or an ecommerce brand shipping out of a Houston garage.

## The Two Main Pricing Models: Hourly vs. Flat Monthly

### Hourly Bookkeeping Rates in Texas

Hourly bookkeepers in Texas typically charge anywhere from $30 to $90 per hour, depending on experience and location. A freelance bookkeeper in a smaller market might sit at the low end; an experienced professional in Austin, Dallas, or Houston with industry specialization will land at the higher end.

Hourly pricing sounds cheap until you realize the incentive problem: the slower the work goes, the bigger your bill. It also makes budgeting nearly impossible. One month you pay $200, the next month a messy quarter costs you $900, and you have no idea why.

Hourly can make sense for small, well-defined projects — say, a one-time cleanup or a few hours of QuickBooks training. For ongoing monthly work, most Texas businesses are better served by flat pricing.

### Flat Monthly Pricing (What Most Firms Use Now)

Modern bookkeeping firms — Balance Bridge included — typically charge a flat monthly fee based on the complexity of your business. You know exactly what you'll pay, the firm is motivated to work efficiently, and there's no surprise invoice after a busy month.

Here's what flat monthly bookkeeping typically looks like for Texas small businesses in 2026:

| Business Profile | Typical Monthly Range | What's Usually Included |
|---|---|---|
| Solo operator or very early startup (1 bank account, low transaction volume) | $300 – $500 | Monthly categorization, bank reconciliation, basic reports |
| Established small business (2–4 accounts, moderate volume, some payroll) | $500 – $1,000 | Everything above, plus AP/AR support, payroll coordination, monthly financial statements |
| Growing business (multiple revenue streams, inventory or job costing, higher volume) | $1,000 – $1,800 | Everything above, plus class/location tracking, cash flow reporting, monthly review calls |
| Complex operations (multiple entities, heavy AP/AR, industry-specific needs) | $1,800 – $2,500+ | Full-service bookkeeping plus controller-level oversight |

These are typical market ranges, not a quote — your actual price depends on the factors below.

## What Actually Drives Your Bookkeeping Price

When a bookkeeping firm quotes you, they're usually weighing five things:

### 1. Transaction Volume

A landscaping business with 80 transactions a month is a very different job than an ecommerce store with 2,000 orders flowing through Shopify, Amazon, and PayPal. More transactions means more categorization, more reconciliation, and more opportunities for something to go sideways.

### 2. Number of Accounts

Every bank account, credit card, loan, and payment processor has to be reconciled monthly. A business with one checking account and one credit card is straightforward. A business with three bank accounts, four cards, a line of credit, Stripe, Square, and Venmo is a bigger lift.

### 3. Payroll and Contractors

If you have employees, someone has to make sure payroll runs correctly, payroll taxes get recorded properly, and everything ties out at year-end. Contractor-heavy businesses (looking at you, Texas construction) add 1099 tracking to the mix.

### 4. Industry Complexity

Some industries just have harder books:

- **Contractors** need job costing so they know which projects actually made money.
- **Restaurants** juggle daily sales reconciliation, tips, high vendor volume, and thin margins.
- **Medical practices** deal with insurance reimbursements that rarely match what was billed.
- **Ecommerce** brands wrestle with sales channels, merchant fees, inventory, and sales tax across states.

### 5. The Current State of Your Books

If your books are current and clean, onboarding is easy. If you're 14 months behind or your QuickBooks file looks like a junk drawer, most firms will quote a one-time cleanup or catch-up project before starting monthly service. That's normal — and worth it. (We wrote a whole guide on catch-up bookkeeping if that's you.)

## In-House Bookkeeper vs. Outsourced Firm: The Real Math

A lot of Texas business owners assume hiring an in-house bookkeeper is the "grown-up" move. Sometimes it is — but run the numbers first.

A full-time bookkeeper in Texas typically earns somewhere in the range of $45,000 to $60,000 a year in salary. Add payroll taxes, benefits, software, training, and the cost of managing them, and the true annual cost often lands well above $60,000 — for one person, with one set of skills, who takes vacations and can quit.

An outsourced firm at $800–$1,500 per month runs $9,600–$18,000 per year and typically comes with a team, documented processes, built-in review, and no gap in coverage when someone is out sick.

The honest crossover point: most businesses don't need a full-time, in-house bookkeeper until they're doing several million in revenue with genuinely daily accounting needs. Below that, you're usually paying full-time money for part-time work.

There's also a hybrid worth mentioning: a part-time or family-member bookkeeper handling day-to-day entry, with an outside firm reviewing monthly and producing the financials. It works — as long as someone with real accounting expertise is checking the work.

## When Bookkeeping Isn't Enough: Upgrading to Controller or CFO Support

Here's a pattern we see constantly: a business grows past $1M–$2M in revenue, the books are clean, the reports arrive on time… and the owner still feels like they're flying blind.

That's not a bookkeeping problem. That's a *decision-support* problem.

- A **bookkeeper** records what happened.
- A **controller** makes sure it's accurate, timely, and organized — and builds the processes around it.
- A **CFO** uses those numbers to answer forward-looking questions: Can we afford that second location? What should we charge? When do we hire?

Fractional controller and CFO services let you buy that expertise by the slice — typically a set number of hours or deliverables per month — instead of paying a six-figure salary. For many Texas businesses, adding fractional controller or CFO support to a solid bookkeeping foundation costs a fraction of one full-time hire and changes how the owner runs the company.

Signals it's time to level up:

- You make pricing, hiring, or expansion decisions on gut feel because the numbers don't tell you anything.
- Your bank or investors are asking for projections you can't produce.
- Cash is tight even though the P&L says you're profitable.
- You're spending Sunday nights building spreadsheets your accounting system should generate.

## How to Compare Quotes Without Getting Burned

When you're shopping bookkeepers, cheap and expensive quotes can both hide problems. Ask every candidate:

1. **What exactly is included?** Monthly reconciliation? Financial statements? AP/AR? Payroll support? A "$300/month" quote that excludes reconciliation isn't cheaper — it's incomplete.
2. **Who actually does the work, and who reviews it?** A second set of eyes catches errors before they compound.
3. **How will you work with my CPA at tax time?** Good bookkeepers hand your tax preparer a clean, reconciled file — which often lowers your tax prep bill too.
4. **What happens if my books are behind or messy?** You want a clear, fixed-scope cleanup plan, not open-ended hourly billing.
5. **When will I get my reports each month?** Financials delivered 45 days late are trivia, not tools.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does bookkeeping cost per month for a small business in Texas?

Most Texas small businesses pay somewhere between $300 and $2,500 per month, depending on transaction volume, number of accounts, payroll needs, and industry complexity. A typical established small business with moderate activity often lands in the $500–$1,000 range.

### Is it cheaper to do my own bookkeeping?

It's cheaper in cash, but rarely in reality. DIY bookkeeping costs you hours every week, and errors — missed deductions, unreconciled accounts, misclassified expenses — tend to surface at tax time, when they're most expensive to fix. Most owners find their time is worth more spent running the business.

### What's the difference between a bookkeeper and a CPA?

A bookkeeper maintains your day-to-day financial records: categorizing transactions, reconciling accounts, and producing financial statements. A CPA is a licensed professional who typically handles tax filing, tax strategy, and attestation work. They're complementary — Balance Bridge keeps your books clean and coordinates with your CPA at tax time (we don't file tax returns ourselves).

### Do bookkeeping firms charge extra for cleanup or catch-up work?

Usually, yes. If your books are behind or disorganized, most firms quote a one-time project fee to bring everything current before monthly service begins. Typical cleanup projects range from a few hundred dollars to several thousand, depending on how far back the mess goes.
